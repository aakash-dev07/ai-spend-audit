# GTM.md

## Exact target user

Not "startups" — specifically: **the engineering manager or CTO at a B2B SaaS startup, 10–50 people, Series A or pre-A, who owns both the engineering budget and the credit card for dev tooling.** They're approving $3–12k/month in AI tool invoices, they've never benchmarked it against anything, and they feel vaguely uneasy about it but haven't made time to audit it.

Secondary user: the **founder wearing the CFO hat at a sub-20-person company.** They're the one who gets the Stripe invoice, doesn't recognize half the line items, and doesn't know if $400/month in Cursor Business seats for 5 engineers is reasonable.

---

## What they Google or scroll through right before they'd want this

- "how much does GitHub Copilot cost per developer"
- "cursor vs github copilot 2025 price comparison"
- "ChatGPT Team vs Enterprise worth it"
- "claude team plan vs pro plan difference"
- Scrolling a tweet thread comparing AI coding tools by cost
- Reading a Hacker News thread titled "We cancelled Copilot — here's what we switched to"
- Looking at their bank statement the morning they get a large Stripe invoice

These are the signals. The user is in cost-comparison mode, not "buy AI tools" mode.

## First 100 users in 30 days with $0

**Week 1: Hacker News Show HN**
"Show HN: I built a free 2-minute audit for AI tool overspend." Post at 9am ET on a Tuesday. This alone can drive 500–2,000 visitors if the copy is sharp. Target: 40–80 audit completions.

**Week 2: Twitter thread**
A thread showing one real audit result ("We were paying $2,400/month for ChatGPT Enterprise with 40 seats. We switched to Claude Team. Same output, $480/month less"). No tool-selling. Just the numbers. Tag the companies whose tools you switched. Realistic reach for a 1k-follower account: 5–15k impressions, 50–200 clicks.

**Week 3: Indie Hackers post**
"I built a free tool to audit AI tool spend — here's what I learned from the first 100 audits." Data-driven. Share surprising findings (e.g., "73% of teams on Enterprise don't use any Enterprise-only features"). Indie Hackers readers love this format. Drives 30–50 more completions.

**Week 4: Direct outreach**
DM 20 engineering managers on LinkedIn/X who've publicly posted about AI tool costs. Offer the tool + a personal 10-minute audit walkthrough. Conversion rate ~20–30% to use the tool.

**Target: 100 audit completions by day 30.** Realistic with this plan.

## The unfair distribution channel

Credex already has relationships with AI infrastructure vendors (Cursor, Anthropic, OpenAI). These vendors have no reason to help users find cheaper alternatives — but they do want more active users. SpendLens can be pitched to vendor partner programs as a "make sure you're on the right plan" tool. A Cursor blog post linking SpendLens as a "is Cursor right for your team?" calculator drives traffic from users who are already high-intent.

This is a channel only Credex could plausibly operate — the conflict of interest is neutralised because Credex is the discount provider, not the original vendor.
